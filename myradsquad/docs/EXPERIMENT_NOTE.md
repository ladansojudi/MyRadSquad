# Experiment note · Paywall A vs B

**Where it runs.** The paywall at the end of onboarding, for new users who joined by
invite. Split 50/50 by account, sticky; same plans (yearly pre-selected, shown per month),
same 3-day trial, same "No thanks".

**A (control) · fun and AI.** Headline and art follow their answer ("Play more,
together." with a game room, "Study together, for real." with a focus room).

**B (variant) · the squad.** "Room for 12." 5 of 5 seats taken, 7 empty, and two friends
who asked to join ("Sam and Priya want in"). No contact list needed: they are friends
who tapped the squad's invite.

**Hypothesis.** B beats A on **day-7 paid conversion per paywall view** because a teen who
arrives by invite (A1) already feels the table is full: friends who can't get in is a
reason they get on day one; features they haven't used aren't. Our research says the
opposite (teens pay for fun and AI), so it's a real bet.

**Deciding metric.** Day-7 paid conversion per paywall view: trials that become paid,
approved Ask to Buy requests included. Day 7, not day 3, so a parent's 24 h approval and
early refunds count. Trial starts alone would reward pressure (44% cancel in 24 h).
~15,200 views per arm to see a +20% lift (2.75% → 3.30%); two to four weeks.

**Side effects to watch.** Ask to Buy decline rate; refunds; plan mix (yearly vs weekly);
invites sent; day-7 activity of users who said no.

**Stop early if** B's Ask to Buy declines run 5+ points above A's, refunds double, anyone
reports the friends B names, or the 50/50 split drifts (broken tracking).

**Next test.** B wins: show the seat moment on Home when a squad fills, before any
paywall. B loses: keep A and test Spark's first spark inside the paywall.
